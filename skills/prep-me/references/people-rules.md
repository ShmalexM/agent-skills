# People rules

The brief says who the user will meet and what those people care about. It is not a dossier.

## Where to look

Use these in order:

1. The calendar invite and the email threads: names, titles, signatures, who introduced whom, and what they said.
2. Local meeting recordings and notes, if the route allows them.
3. The public web: the person's employer page, talks, podcasts, articles, patents, public code and press.
4. LinkedIn through public search results only. Use the title and snippet that a search engine shows, or a public page that opens without a login.
5. Anything the user supplies: a pasted profile, a saved profile PDF or a file in the route's folder.

## LinkedIn

Never log in to LinkedIn. Never drive the user's browser session to view profiles. Never run a scraper or a data broker tool. This protects the user's account.

If LinkedIn is the only useful source for a person, list that person with their status as `unconfirmed`. Then ask for one thing in the hand-over: "Open this profile and save it as a PDF to `<folder>`." Give the search URL when you can. When the user supplies the file, read it and update the brief.

## Confirm identity

Many people share a name. Mark a person `confirmed` only when two independent facts match. For example, a public page shows the same name, the same employer and the same title as the email signature. Otherwise, mark the person `unconfirmed` and say what is missing. Never use a photo or a detail from a profile that you have not confirmed.

## What to keep

Keep what helps the user work with this person:

- Current role, how long they have held it and earlier roles that matter.
- What they have said in public about the topic.
- What they said to the user, with a reference to it.
- Shared history, such as a shared employer, school or contact, when a source shows it.

## What to leave out

Leave out health, family, religion, politics, age, money, home location and private social media. Do not guess at personality. Say "this person has written twice about X" and not "this person is analytical". If you cannot see what a person cares about, say so.
