# Prep types

Pick one type. Each one sets the win condition, what to research hardest and what the sections focus on. If the event is a mix, pick the type that decides the outcome and borrow from the others.

## Interview

- **Win condition.** The user moves to the next round, and also learns if they want this job.
- **Round first.** Find which round this is and who runs it. A recruiter screen, a hiring manager, a panel and an executive each test different things. Write the brief for this round only, and put later rounds in one short note.
- **Research hardest.** What the person who runs this round cares about, the role's real problem, how the company makes money, recent changes in the company, and what happened to the last person or team in this seat if that is public.
- **You in this.** A short opening answer, one real customer delivery story with a problem and a result, and the true gaps with an honest way to handle each one. A prototype is not a delivery story, so call it a prototype.
- **Prompts for the user.** The agent cannot write these. List them under "Do first": why this company, why move now, one failure story that is safe to tell, and what the user already told the recruiter.
- **Say and don't say.** Use only facts that the resume or the route allows, in the words of the version the interviewer has. Add a one-line deflection for pay and a boundary line for what the user cannot discuss. Check any public case study that a line points to, because the interviewer can read it. Do not frame the interviewer's own business as the bad outcome.
- **Questions.** Two sets: the hardest questions they may ask, and questions the user can ask that show thought and test the job.

## Business meeting

- **Win condition.** The one decision or next step that the user wants when the meeting ends. If the user has not said, state your best guess and mark it as an assumption.
- **Research hardest.** Who decides, what each person wants, past promises, what changed since the last contact, and what the other side is likely to ask for.
- **You in this.** What the user and their team bring, past dealings with these people, and what the user should not offer without approval.
- **Say and don't say.** The opening line, the ask, likely objections with answers, and topics that are not yet safe to raise.
- **Questions.** Questions that expose the real blocker or the real budget.

## Customer deep-dive

Use this for a proof of value, a demo or an account review. It reads work sources, so keep it on a work route.

- **Win condition.** The customer's next commitment, and any risk found early.
- **Research hardest.** Account history, open commitments on both sides, what is deployed now, stakeholders and who is new, and competing products or internal teams.
- **You in this.** What the user personally owns or promised on this account.
- **Say and don't say.** What each stakeholder needs to hear, and what the other customers' names and data must never touch.
- **Questions.** Questions that test if the customer's success measure is real and if the champion still has power.

## Negotiation

Use this for an offer, comp, contract or vendor deal.

- **Win condition.** The result that the user wants, the result they can accept, and the point where they walk away. Ask the user for the walk-away point if no source gives it. Do not invent it.
- **Research hardest.** Market data with dates and sources, the other side's limits and pressures, their likely first position, and what they can give that costs them little.
- **You in this.** The user's real alternatives and what makes them strong or weak.
- **Say and don't say.** The first line, how to respond to the likely first offer, and what not to reveal, such as a current salary or a real deadline. Check local rules before you state what a person may legally be asked. Say that this is not legal advice.
- **Questions.** Questions that reveal their limits, and questions the user must answer alone before the call.

## Talk or event

Use this for a conference, panel, speaker series or trade show.

- **Win condition.** What the audience should do or remember, or who the user must meet.
- **Research hardest.** The audience, the other speakers, what was said last year, the current debate in this field, and the people worth meeting with a reason for each.
- **You in this.** What only the user can say from their own experience.
- **Say and don't say.** An outline, the opening line, and short intros for the people to meet. Mark claims that are not cleared for a public stage.
- **Questions.** The hardest questions from the audience, and questions to ask other speakers.

## Plain topic

Use this for "get me up to speed on X" with no event attached.

- **Win condition.** The user can explain the topic in plain words and form an opinion on it.
- **Research hardest.** The core mechanism, the players, the current state with dates, and what people who know the field argue about.
- **You in this.** Where the topic touches the user's work and what they might do about it.
- **Sections.** Leave out "People" and "Say and don't say" unless named people matter. Set `prep_type: topic` so that the check allows this.
- **Questions.** Questions that test the mechanism and the user's own position.
- If the user only wants a queue of things to read later, use the `learn-later` skill. If they want one concept taught with a live widget, use the `explain-concept` skill.
